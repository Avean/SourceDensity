\begin{itemize}
    \item Hydra as a classical model organism for regeneration and pattern formation.
    \item Head regeneration and the emergence of localized organizer/head signals.
    \item Activator-inhibitor modelling tradition in Hydra.
    \item Gierer--Meinhardt models and their role in explaining head formation.
    \item Ambiguity: several different mathematical systems are called Gierer--Meinhardt-type models.
    \item Importance of distinguishing:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item models with constitutive production;
        \item models without constitutive production;
        \item models with source-dependent production;
        \item models with prescribed source density;
        \item models with dynamic source density.
    \end{itemize}
    \item Biological problem: regeneration requires wound response. Therefore, a reasonable model should allow a stable resting/no-head state and activation only after sufficiently strong perturbation.
    \item Pattern formation problem: classical diffusion-driven instability and Turing mechanisms can generate peaks, but often select peak locations by domain geometry and unstable modes.
    \item Positional memory problem: grafting and polarization experiments suggest that tissue fragments remember where they came from.
    \item Source density/source gradient as a candidate mechanism for positional information.
    \item Main questions of the paper:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item What does source density add to Gierer--Meinhardt-type Hydra models?
        \item Is it needed for regeneration?
        \item Is it needed for positional memory?
        \item Is it enough to stabilize heads at arbitrary graft-induced locations?
    \end{itemize}
    \item Outline of contributions:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item systematic audit of Gierer--Meinhardt variants;
        \item comparison of stability and diffusion-driven instability properties;
        \item comparison of regeneration and pattern formation with and without source density;
        \item analysis of source density as positional memory;
        \item proposal of a head identity variable with hysteresis.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
